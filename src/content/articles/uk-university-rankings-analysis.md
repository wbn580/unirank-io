UK universities consistently dominate the top 10: Oxford (#1 THE, #3 QS), Cambridge (#5 QS), Imperial College London (#2 QS). The Russell Group (24 research-intensive universities) accounts for nearly all UK universities in the global top 200. Post-1992 universities — former polytechnics — are improving but rarely enter the top 500 globally.

UK universities' strength: research output (high citations per paper, strong international collaboration), teaching reputation (Oxford/Cambridge tutorial system is world-renowned). Weakness: declining staff/student ratios, dependence on international student fees (financial sustainability risk if visa policies restrict international recruitment), Brexit impact on EU research funding and academic mobility.

For international students, UK rankings offer prestige at a cost: the 3-year degree structure means lower total cost despite high annual fees. The Graduate Route visa (2-year post-study work) has made the UK more attractive relative to Australia and Canada, which offer longer post-study work rights.
