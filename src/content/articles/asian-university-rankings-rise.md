Asian universities are the fastest-rising cohort in global rankings. The trend is driven by massive investment in research (China's Double First Class initiative, Singapore's RIE 2025 plan), improving English-language publication output, and increasing numbers of international faculty and students.

Key movers: National University of Singapore (NUS) at #8 (QS) is Asia's top-ranked university. Tsinghua (#14) and Peking (#20) are the first Chinese universities in the top 20. KAIST (Korea), University of Tokyo, and HKU remain in the top 50. NTU Singapore, Fudan, Shanghai Jiao Tong, and Zhejiang University are approaching the top 50.

The trend is supply-side: Asian governments invest proportionally more in higher education R&D than Western governments. China's research output surpassed the US in volume in 2020 and is approaching parity in citation impact. If current trends continue, Asian universities will hold 30–40% of top-100 positions by 2035 (currently ~20%). For international students, Asian universities offer rising prestige at substantially lower cost than US/UK equivalents.
