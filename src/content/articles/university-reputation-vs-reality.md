University reputation — the core of QS and a major THE component — measures what academics think of each other's institutions. It does not measure teaching quality, student satisfaction, or graduate outcomes.

The evidence: The UK's National Student Survey (NSS) consistently shows that some post-1992 universities (former polytechnics) score higher on teaching quality than Russell Group universities. The Australian QILT Student Experience Survey shows regional universities (UNE, CQU, Southern Cross) often outscore Go8 universities on teaching quality and student support. Research prestige and teaching quality are weakly correlated — a Nobel laureate may be a terrible lecturer, and a university with no research stars may have excellent teachers.

For undergraduates, "student satisfaction," "teaching quality," and "learning resources" scores (available in QILT, NSS, and Niche) are more directly relevant than research rankings. The most elite universities trade on reputation but do not consistently deliver the best undergraduate education.
