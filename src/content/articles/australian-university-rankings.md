Australia has 7 universities in the QS top 100 — the highest per capita of any country. Melbourne (#13), Sydney (#18), UNSW (#19), ANU (#30), Monash (#37), Queensland (#40), UWA (#77). The Group of Eight dominates, but the Australian Technology Network universities (UTS, RMIT, Curtin, QUT, UniSA) have been rising rapidly — UTS entered the QS top 100 in 2024.

Australian universities' strength: research citations (relatively small, efficient research operations), international student ratio (among the highest globally, contributing to international outlook scores), employer reputation (Australian graduates are well-regarded in Asia-Pacific). Weakness: teaching metrics (large class sizes compared to elite US/UK institutions), faculty/student ratio (worse than UK universities).

For international students, Australia's ranking density — 7 in the top 100 from a population of 26M — provides high-quality options across price points. Melbourne and Sydney are the most expensive; regional Go8 universities (Adelaide, UWA) offer similar prestige at lower cost.
