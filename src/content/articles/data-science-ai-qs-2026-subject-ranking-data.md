The 2026 QS World University Rankings by Subject for Data Science and Artificial Intelligence places Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) at rank 1, with 201 institutions from 37 countries and regions appearing in the global table as of the April 3, 2026 data cut. The median rank across the full list sits at 101.0, meaning half of the ranked programs fall outside the top 100.

## How the 201 ranked programs are distributed across countries

The United States holds the largest share of the list with 41 ranked institutions, more than double the next country. The United Kingdom follows with 22, while China (Mainland) and Australia each contribute 13. India and Spain each have 8, and Canada, Italy, and Malaysia each place 7. Hong Kong SAR, China rounds out the top ten countries with 6.

This distribution tells a clear story about where the field's academic capacity concentrates. The US presence is not just large in absolute terms — it also spans the full range of the ranking, from the very top to the middle of the table. The UK's 22 programs make it the second-largest contributor, while the relatively high counts for Australia and Malaysia suggest that data science and AI education has become a significant export sector for these countries, not merely an add-on to existing computer science departments.

## What separates the top tier from the rest

The score bands reveal a striking discontinuity. Eleven institutions score 90 or above, and 24 more fall in the 80–89 range. That gives 35 programs with scores of 80 or higher. But the next band — 70–79 — contains only 15 institutions. In other words, the gap between the 35th and 50th ranked programs is substantial: the top 50 programs are not evenly spread but rather cluster into a high-scoring elite and a middle group that drops off noticeably.

The rank bands confirm this pattern. The top 10 ranks are filled by 10 institutions, ranks 11–20 by another 10, and ranks 21–30 by 10 more. The 31–50 band holds 20 institutions, and ranks 51–100 contain 50. From rank 101 to 150, the list expands to 101 institutions — meaning more than half of all ranked programs sit between 101 and 150. No institutions appear beyond rank 150; the list stops at 201 total entries.

The practical takeaway for prospective students: the top 30 programs are tightly clustered in score terms, but the drop from the top 50 into the 51–100 range is where the ranking becomes more about institutional breadth than about a narrow set of elite performers.

## Data notes

This analysis is based on the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026 for Data Science and Artificial Intelligence, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data reference date of April 3, 2026. The ranking covers 60 subject tables in total; this article uses only the Data Science and Artificial Intelligence table. The global list includes 201 institutions across 37 countries and regions.

The ranking methodology incorporates Academic Reputation, Employer Reputation, Citations per paper, H-index, and International Research Network scores. The scores shown in the table are the overall scores that determine rank order. Ranks are assigned as the lower-bound integer of the computed position; ties are resolved by the ranking algorithm and are not broken by score alone.

The country leaderboard counts each institution once, based on the country or region listed in the ranking data. Institutions with multiple campuses or joint programs are counted under their primary country of affiliation. The median rank of 101.0 is the midpoint of the ordered list of 201 institutions; it does not represent an average score or a threshold for inclusion in any particular tier.

The rank bands and score bands presented here are derived from the published ranking data and reflect the distribution as of the April 3, 2026 data cut. No projections or estimates are made beyond the published figures.
