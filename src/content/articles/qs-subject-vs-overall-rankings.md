QS subject rankings are more actionable than overall rankings for students who know their intended field. Overall rankings measure institutional prestige; subject rankings measure discipline-specific reputation, citations, and employer perception within a field.

QS ranks 55 narrow subjects and 5 broad subject areas. Subject rankings use a discipline-specific weighting: Academic Reputation is always the largest component but Research Citations per Paper is weighted higher in life sciences (where citations are the primary currency) and lower in arts and humanities (where books and performances are important outputs). H-index (measuring research productivity and impact) is weighted in life sciences and engineering but not in arts or social sciences.

For a student choosing between two universities, subject rankings are 3–5× more informative than overall rankings. A philosophy student choosing between Pittsburgh (QS #150 overall, #1–2 Philosophy) and ANU (QS #30 overall, #3 Philosophy) should use subject rankings — the discipline-specific prestige and faculty quality are captured in the subject ranking.
