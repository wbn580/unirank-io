## Understanding the GRE Verbal Section and Your Starting Point

Before diving into a study plan, it’s important to recognize that the GRE Verbal section measures complex reasoning skills rather than straightforward vocabulary knowledge. The Text Completion and Reading Comprehension question types require not only a strong vocabulary but also the ability to analyze sentence structure, discern tone, and draw inferences from academic passages. Your current skill level—whether you are a beginner, intermediate, or advanced test-taker—will determine the intensity and focus of your daily work over a 3–6 month period.

## Sample 3-Month Daily Schedule for an Intermediate Learner

A structured daily routine helps build consistency. For an intermediate learner with a baseline score around 150–155, a typical day might look like this:

- **Morning (1.5 hours):** Review 30 high-frequency GRE words using flashcards and practice 5 Text Completion questions, analyzing each answer choice.
- **Afternoon (1.5 hours):** Complete one Reading Comprehension passage with all its questions, then dissect the passage for main idea, tone, and structure.
- **Evening (1 hour):** Error analysis of the day’s wrong answers and targeted practice on identified weaknesses.

This schedule assumes 4 hours of daily study. Adjust the duration based on your available time, but maintain the sequence of learning new material, applying it, and reviewing mistakes.

## Adapting the Plan for Different Skill Levels

Every GRE aspirant starts from a different baseline. Below are general guidelines to tailor the daily tasks:

![A calendar and study materials for a GRE preparation plan.](https://img.unirank.io/留学-大学排名/3-6-gre-2026-07-24-a2f314fba6-image-01-2026-1880x1253.jpg)


- **Beginners (diagnostic score below 150):** Dedicate the first month to building vocabulary (30–50 words per day) and understanding question formats. Spend 2 hours daily on vocabulary and basic sentence equivalence drills, and 1 hour on untimed Reading Comprehension practice.
- **Intermediate learners (150–160):** Follow the balanced schedule above, gradually reducing vocabulary time and increasing passage complexity.
- **Advanced learners (160+):** Focus on high-difficulty questions and timed sections. Limit vocabulary review to 15–20 challenging words daily and spend most sessions on dense, graduate-level passages.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many months do I need to prepare for the GRE?
The ideal preparation period depends on your starting score and target score. A 3-month plan can work for those with a strong foundation, while 6 months allows for a more gradual buildup of vocabulary and reading skills.

### What is the hardest part of GRE Verbal?
Many test-takers find Text Completion challenging because it requires precise vocabulary knowledge and an understanding of sentence logic. Reading Comprehension demands the ability to process complex academic prose under time pressure.

### Should I study every day?
Consistency is key. A daily study habit, even if only for 1–2 hours, is more effective than long, irregular sessions. Most plans include one rest day per week to avoid burnout.

### How do I improve my reading comprehension skills?
Practice with dense, academic-style articles from sources like academic journals or high-quality publications. Focus on identifying the author’s main argument, the structure of the passage, and the function of each paragraph.
