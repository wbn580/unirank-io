The Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings by Subject, released annually each October, now evaluate performance across 11 broad subject areas, from Arts and Humanities to Clinical and Health. In the 2025 edition, THE assessed 1,907 institutions globally for its overall ranking, but the subject-level breakdowns often draw a more granular picture: for example, the University of Oxford retained the top spot in Clinical and Health for the 13th consecutive year, while the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) claimed first place in both Business and Economics and Social Sciences. These subject-specific tables are not simply the overall ranking sliced by discipline; they employ recalibrated weightings—research environment, teaching environment, research quality, industry income, and international outlook—that differ across subjects. For instance, in Engineering, the research quality indicator carries a 37.5% weight, whereas in Arts and Humanities, teaching environment is weighted at 37.5% and research quality at 25.0% [THE 2025 Subject Rankings Methodology]. Understanding these methodological distinctions is critical for applicants and parents aiming to match program strengths with career goals, rather than relying on a university’s aggregate global rank.

## The Five Pillars and Their Subject-Specific Calibration

Each THE subject ranking rests on the same five **performance pillars** as the overall World University Rankings: Teaching (the learning environment), Research Environment (volume, income, and reputation), Research Quality (citation impact, research strength, excellence, and influence), Industry Income (innovation and knowledge transfer), and International Outlook (staff, students, and research). However, the **weight assigned to each pillar** shifts according to the nature of the discipline.

For Clinical and Health subjects, Research Quality receives the highest weighting at 37.5%, reflecting the field’s reliance on clinical trials and publication impact. In contrast, Arts and Humanities allocate 37.5% to Teaching and only 25.0% to Research Quality [THE 2025 Subject Methodology]. Social Sciences balance Teaching (32.5%) and Research Quality (30.0%), while Engineering prioritizes Research Quality at 37.5% and Teaching at 30.0%. This recalibration means a university ranked 50th overall might rank 20th in Engineering if its citation impact is strong, or 80th in Arts if its teaching environment is weaker. Users should never assume subject rankings mirror the overall league table.

### Reading the Subject-Specific Indicator Scores

Beyond the overall subject score, THE provides **individual indicator scores** for each of the 13 underlying metrics. For example, in the 2025 subject tables for Computer Science, the indicator “Citations per Paper” is part of Research Quality but is normalized by subject field to account for different citation densities. A university may score 92.4 on Teaching but only 68.7 on Industry Income in the same subject. These granular scores allow applicants to identify whether a program excels in research output, teaching resources, or industry partnerships.

## Decoding the Research Quality Component

The Research Quality pillar is often the most opaque to first-time readers. THE uses three sub-metrics: **Citation Impact** (field-weighted), **Research Strength** (the 75th percentile of field-weighted citation impact), and **Research Excellence** (the proportion of papers in the top 10% of journals by citation impact). For the 2025 subject rankings, THE analyzed over 134 million citations across 16.5 million publications [THE 2025 World University Rankings Data].

In subjects like Physical Sciences, where publication volume is high, Research Strength and Excellence can differentiate institutions with similar overall scores. For instance, in the 2025 Physical Sciences table, the California Institute of Technology scored 99.9 on Research Excellence despite a lower Teaching score of 81.2, pushing it to 6th place globally. Conversely, a university with high Teaching but low Research Excellence may rank lower in research-intensive fields. Applicants targeting PhD programs should prioritize universities with high Research Excellence scores in their intended subject.

### How Field-Weighted Citation Impact Works

THE normalizes citation counts by subject field and year, producing a **Field-Weighted Citation Impact (FWCI)** score. An FWCI of 1.0 represents world average; scores above 1.0 indicate above-average impact. In the 2025 subject tables, the top 10 universities in Life Sciences all had FWCI values above 1.8, while the top 10 in Arts and Humanities averaged 1.5. This normalization prevents citation-heavy fields like biomedicine from dominating humanities rankings.

## International Outlook and Its Subject Variations

The International Outlook pillar comprises three metrics: proportion of international students, proportion of international staff, and international co-authorship. In the 2025 subject rankings, the **weight of International Outlook** varies from 5.0% in Clinical and Health to 10.0% in Arts and Humanities and Social Sciences [THE 2025 Subject Methodology]. This means that for humanities programs, a diverse campus environment can boost a university’s subject rank more significantly than for medical schools.

For example, in the 2025 Arts and Humanities table, the University of Hong Kong ranked 30th globally, partly driven by an International Outlook score of 98.4—the highest among the top 50—reflecting its high proportion of international faculty and students. In contrast, a domestic-focused institution with strong teaching but low international diversity may see its subject rank suppressed in fields where International Outlook carries more weight. Students valuing global exposure should check this indicator before applying.

## Industry Income: A Subject-Specific Metric

Industry Income measures a university’s ability to generate income from industry research partnerships and knowledge transfer. The weight for this pillar is **5.0% in most subjects** but rises to 10.0% in Engineering and Technology, and 7.5% in Physical Sciences [THE 2025 Subject Methodology]. This reflects the expectation that engineering programs collaborate heavily with industry.

In the 2025 Engineering subject table, the top-ranked institution (MIT) scored 100.0 on Industry Income, while the University of Cambridge scored 85.3 despite ranking 3rd overall. A university like Tsinghua University, ranked 12th in Engineering, achieved an Industry Income score of 99.1, indicating strong corporate partnerships. ## Comparing Subject Rankings Across Different Publishers

THE subject rankings are one of four major global systems, each with distinct methodologies. The **QS World University Rankings by Subject** (2025 edition) evaluates 55 individual subjects using academic reputation, employer reputation, and research citations per paper, with no industry income component. The **U.S. News Best Global Universities Subject Rankings** (2024–2025) uses 13 indicators including global research reputation, regional research reputation, and normalized citation impact, but weights vary by subject. The **Academic Ranking of World Universities (ARWU) by Subject** (2024) relies solely on objective indicators: publication count, citation impact, and international collaboration.

For example, in the field of Computer Science, the 2025 THE subject ranking placed MIT 1st, while QS ranked MIT 5th and ARWU ranked MIT 3rd. These discrepancies arise from different weightings: QS gives 40% weight to academic reputation, whereas THE allocates 37.5% to Research Quality. Students should cross-reference at least two ranking systems to get a balanced view. A university that ranks high across all three likely has both strong reputation and research output.

### Using Subject Tables for Course-Level Decisions

Subject-level rankings are not equivalent to individual course quality. A university ranked 10th in THE’s Business and Economics table may offer a weak undergraduate program in finance if its strength lies in economics research. THE does not rank individual courses or departments. Students should drill down to the **specific indicator scores** for Teaching and Research Quality within a subject, then visit departmental websites for curriculum details. For instance, the 2025 THE subject table for Law shows the University of Cambridge scoring 94.3 on Teaching and 97.8 on Research Quality, but its LLM program structure may differ from the University of Melbourne, which scored 76.5 and 82.1 respectively.

## FAQ

### Q1: How often are THE subject rankings updated, and when is the best time to use them for applications?

THE releases its subject rankings annually in October, alongside the overall World University Rankings. For the 2025 cycle, the subject tables were published on October 16, 2024. Applicants should use the most recent edition when applying for the following academic year—e.g., the 2025 rankings are relevant for Fall 2026 admissions. Because rankings can shift by 5–10 positions year-over-year, relying on data older than 18 months may misrepresent a program’s current standing.

### Q2: Which subject ranking weight is most important for STEM fields compared to humanities?

For STEM fields (Engineering, Physical Sciences, Life Sciences), the **Research Quality** pillar typically carries the highest weight (37.5% in Engineering and Physical Sciences). For humanities (Arts and Humanities, Law), **Teaching** is weighted at 37.5%, making teaching environment more influential. In Social Sciences, Teaching (32.5%) and Research Quality (30.0%) are nearly balanced. Applicants should prioritize the pillar that aligns with their goals: research-oriented students should favor high Research Quality scores, while teaching-focused students should examine Teaching indicator scores.

### Q3: How can I find a specific university’s subject rank if it is not listed in the top 100?

THE publishes subject rankings for the top 100–400 institutions per subject, depending on the field. If a university is not listed, its subject score is below the threshold—for example, in 2025, the 400th-ranked institution in Engineering had an overall score of approximately 18.0 out of 100. Users can access the full subject table via the THE website and filter by region or country. Alternatively, the THE data portal allows downloading of all scored institutions for a given subject, even if unranked.
