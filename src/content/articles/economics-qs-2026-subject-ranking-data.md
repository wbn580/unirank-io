The QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026 lists 699 institutions worldwide in Economics & Econometrics, spanning 75 countries and territories. Harvard University holds the top position. For students selecting a program by subject, the key question is not just who ranks first, but how the discipline's global landscape is structured—and where the concentration of quality actually lies.

## The Global Footprint: 699 Institutions Across 75 Countries

The 2026 edition of the QS subject rankings for Economics & Econometrics covers 699 ranked institutions globally. This is a substantial field, but it is not evenly distributed. A median rank cannot be computed for the listed institutions: the institution at the median position has only a published rank range (351–400), so no single median figure is available.

The geographic concentration is striking. The United States leads with 110 ranked institutions, more than any other country. The United Kingdom follows with 59, and China (Mainland) ranks third with 52. Germany contributes 32, while France and India each place 27 institutions. Italy has 23, Australia 22, Spain 20, and Canada 18. Together, these ten countries account for the vast majority of the ranked field, and for a student choosing a destination, the practical implication is clear: the depth of options varies enormously by country.

## The Top Tier: Where the Highest Scores Cluster

At the very top of the table, the concentration of elite programs is tight. The top 10 positions are held by 10 institutions, and the top 20 by 20. The top 30 contains 30 programs, and the top 50 includes 50. The top 100 threshold is crossed by 101 institutions—one more than a strict count of 100, reflecting the way tied ranks are handled in the QS methodology.

What distinguishes this upper band is not just rank but score. The QS subject rankings use a composite score that draws on academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, and other indicators. In the 2026 edition, the highest overall scores sit in the 90s: 10 institutions score between 90 and 99. A further 20 institutions fall in the 80–89 band. Among the 202 institutions with a published overall score, 30 score 80 or above. The remaining 497 listed institutions are published with a rank or rank band but no overall score, and a missing score does not mean they scored below any threshold.

The score distribution becomes more telling as you move down. The 70–79 band contains 99 institutions, and the 60–69 band contains 73. The published score bands cover only institutions whose overall score was released, and they stop at the 60–69 band. This reflects the way QS publishes scores: an overall score is released only for a leading subset of ranked institutions, and the lowest published score band is 60–69. Institutions without a published score are still ranked; a missing score does not mean a low score or removal from the ranking. For a reader comparing programs, a score in the 60s is the lowest published score band, and it applies only to institutions whose overall score was released; the 497 institutions without a published score are still ranked and are not covered by any score band.

## Rank Distribution: How Many Programs Sit at Each Level

The rank bands tell a complementary story. There are 10 institutions in the rank 1–10 band, 10 in the 11–20 band, and 10 in the 21–30 band. The 31–50 band holds 20 institutions, and the 51–100 band holds 51. From there, the numbers swell: the 101–150 band contains 49 institutions, and the 151–200 band contains 52. For some institutions QS publishes only a rank range rather than an exact rank, so the 201–300 range is not reported as a single band; the published ranges covering it are 201–250 with 49 institutions and 251–300 with 48. Outside the top 300, there are 400 institutions. Among the published rank bands, the largest single band is 551–700 with 149 institutions.

This distribution has a practical implication for prospective students. If your target is a program ranked in the top 50, you are looking at a field of 50 institutions globally. If your target is the top 100, the field expands to 101. But if you are willing to consider programs ranked beyond 300, you are looking at hundreds of options—many of which may be perfectly good departments that simply do not have the same international reputation profile as the leaders.

## The Country Leaderboard: Where the Programs Are

The country-level view reinforces the concentration at the top. The United States places 110 institutions in the 2026 ranking, the United Kingdom 59, and China (Mainland) 52. Germany follows with 32, France and India with 27 each, Italy with 23, Australia with 22, Spain with 20, and Canada with 18.

For a student choosing a country, these numbers are not just counts—they are indicators of the breadth of options. A country with 110 ranked programs offers a much wider range of selectivity levels, research environments, and regional placements than a country with 18. That said, a higher count does not automatically mean a higher concentration of top-tier programs. The United States, for example, has both the largest number of ranked institutions and the top-ranked program, but the distribution of its 110 programs across the rank bands is not uniform.

## What the Indicators Mean for Program Choice

The QS subject ranking for Economics & Econometrics is built on several component scores, including academic reputation, employer reputation, and citations. The overall scores that appear in the ranking are composites of these indicators, and they are not directly comparable across subjects—a score of 80 in Economics & Econometrics does not mean the same thing as a score of 80 in a different discipline.

For students, the most useful reading of the data is not the exact rank number but the band in which a program falls. The difference between rank 10 and rank 20 is meaningful, but the difference between rank 101 and rank 150 is less so—both fall in the same broad tier of the ranking. The score bands provide a more stable comparison: a program scoring in the 90s is in the very top tier, while a program scoring in the 70s is still strong but not at the same international level.
